APR1400 Design Features — What Changed from the OPR1000 (Power, Design Life, Seismic, Safety Injection, MMIS, Severe Accidents)

How the 1,400 MWe evolutionary PWR moved from the OPR1000: 3,983 MWt, 60 years, SSE 0.3 g, four DVI trains, an in-containment water tank and a fully digital control room.

Concept C-PE-002
Definition

The APR1400 is a 1,400 MWe-class evolutionary pressurized water reactor developed to the Korean Utility Requirements Document (KURD) on the basis of OPR1000 (Korean Standard Nuclear Plant) operating experience and the US System 80+ design. It keeps the basic two-loop configuration and raises power, design life, seismic capacity, safety injection, instrumentation and control, and severe-accident response by one step each.

Development began in 1992, and the South Korean standard design approval was granted in May 2002. The first unit, Shin-Kori 3 (now Saeul 1), received its operating license in October 2015, and in the United States the design was certified in 2019 (10 CFR Part 52, Appendix F). Note first that it is an evolutionary design based on active safety systems, not a passive design.

Principle and mechanism

Requirements drive the design — the KURD targets

The APR1400's design features follow from the utility requirements set early in development. The main values of the KURD, which was prepared with reference to the US EPRI ALWR requirements, are:

  • Thermal power 4,000 MWt (NSSS), design life 60 years, SSE 0.3 g
  • Core damage frequency (CDF) below 10⁻⁵/RY, containment failure frequency (CFF) below 10⁻⁶/RY
  • Core thermal margin of 10 % or more, at least 30 minutes for operator action, SBO coping of 8 hours or more
  • Availability 90 %, occupational dose below 1 man-Sv/RY, fewer than 0.8 unplanned trips per year

The design PSA results are CDF 2.25×10⁻⁶/RY and CFF 2.84×10⁻⁷/RY, inside the targets (KHNP presentation). The CDF and CFF targets are utility performance goals and sit at a different level from the safety goals in the NSSC notice.

Primary system — the same two loops, larger margins

The piping arrangement is the same as the OPR1000 (two loops, each with one hot leg, two cold legs, one SG and two RCPs). What changed is the margin.

  • Hot-leg temperature 621 °F → 615 °F: more operating margin and less stress corrosion in SG tubes
  • Larger pressurizer: absorbs transients up to Condition III without a reactor trip
  • SGs: Alloy 690TT tubes (13,102 per SG), tube-plugging margin 8 → 10 %, more secondary-side inventory for a longer dry-out time
  • Core of 241 assemblies (16×16 PLUS7), 93 CEAs, cycle of 18 months or more, thermal margin of 10 % or more
  • The spring safety valves on top of the pressurizer and the separate SDS valves are combined into four POSRVs (set pressure 2,470 psia, 244.9 t/h each).

Safety injection — from two cold-leg trains to four direct vessel injection trains

The OPR1000 injects water from the RWT outside containment into the cold legs with two HPSI and two LPSI pumps. When the RWT empties, a recirculation actuation (RAS) is needed to switch suction to the containment sump. The APR1400 changed this as follows.

  • Four mechanically independent trains: one SIP and one SIT per train, with the cross-connecting piping (common header) removed. Electrically there are two divisions (two EDGs, each supplying two SIPs).
  • Direct vessel injection (DVI): water goes straight into the upper downcomer of the reactor vessel, reducing the path by which injection water spills out of a cold-leg break.
  • SIT fluidic device: while the water level is above the standpipe, flow is high; below it, vortex resistance gives a low flow for a long time (about 200 seconds) → the large LPSI pumps are eliminated.
  • IRWST: the refueling water is kept in an annular tank inside containment and serves as the single source for safety injection, containment spray and shutdown cooling. Spilled water returns to the IRWST, so there is no recirculation switchover, and the tank also serves as the quench tank for POSRV steam discharge and as the water source for reactor cavity flooding.

The shutdown cooling system was separated from safety injection, and its design pressure was raised to 900 psig against interfacing-system LOCA.

Auxiliary feedwater and power supply

Auxiliary feedwater has two divisions, each with one 100 % motor-driven pump, one 100 % turbine-driven pump and a dedicated auxiliary feedwater storage tank. For emergency power, the South Korean APR1400 has two EDGs (8,000 kW at Saeul 1 and 2) plus a 7,200 kW AAC-DG per unit, while the US DC has four EDGs plus a gas-turbine AAC. This is what the "strengthened requirements such as AAC installation" in the 2010 exam, session 2, Q1 refers to.

Instrumentation and control — a fully digital MMIS

The main control room consists of a large display panel (LDP), operator workstations, soft controls, computer-based procedures and a safety console for essential safety functions. Safety I&C (PPS, CPCS, ESF-CCS) runs on a four-channel PLC platform, and a DPS on a different platform guards against software common-cause failure (two channels with 2-out-of-2 logic in South Korea; four FPGA channels with 2-out-of-4 logic in the US DC).

Severe-accident response

Dedicated features were provided to reflect the 2001 severe accident policy and SECY-93-087: rapid depressurization through the POSRVs (to 250 psia or below), 30 PARs plus 10 igniters, the cavity flooding system (IRWST → HVT → cavity), a large reactor cavity (0.02 m²/MWt), and the emergency containment spray backup system (ECSBS).

Building layout (73rd exam, session 3, Q6)

The APR1400 places twin units side by side (slide-along) with shared buildings between them. The reactor building and auxiliary building sit on a common basemat, which raises seismic resistance and reduces the walls between buildings. The auxiliary building surrounds the reactor building, and the four SIPs are placed one per quadrant so that fire, flooding or sabotage cannot spread between trains; the two EDGs are also separated in compartments on opposite sides. The turbine building is placed so that the reactor building lies on the extension of the turbine axis, reducing the turbine-missile risk. The equipment hatch (inside diameter 7.8 m) is sized for one-piece SG replacement.

Key points at a glance

  1. Definition: a 1,400 MWe-class evolutionary PWR based on the OPR1000 and System 80+, built to the KURD (two loops, four RCPs).
  2. Requirements: 60 years, SSE 0.3 g, CDF < 10⁻⁵ and CFF < 10⁻⁶/RY, thermal margin ≥ 10 %, SBO 8 h.
  3. Primary system: 615 °F, larger pressurizer and SGs, Alloy 690TT, four POSRVs (safety valve and depressurization combined).
  4. Safety injection: four mechanical trains with DVI, SIT fluidic device (no LPSI), IRWST (no recirculation switchover).
  5. Power: two EDGs + AAC-DG (US DC: four EDGs + gas turbine).
  6. I&C: fully digital MMIS (LDP, soft controls, computerized procedures), four-channel PPS + DPS.
  7. Severe accidents: POSRV depressurization, PARs and igniters, CFS, ECSBS.
  8. Layout: common basemat, SI pumps in separate quadrants, turbine-axis orientation.
  9. Saeul 3 and 4: aircraft impact walls 4.5 ft, ERDV, LCS cavity, 24 h batteries.

This is a model answer, not an official solution. It reflects the criteria current as of 2026.

Exam relevance

The APR1400 has appeared five times in the Korean Professional Engineer (Nuclear Power) exam: the 65th exam, session 1, Q6 (short answer: APR1400, Advanced Power Reactor 1400); the 73rd exam, session 3, Q6 (building layouts of KSNP, KSNP+ and APR1400 compared); 2010 exam, session 2, Q1 (the severe accident policy and the main APR1400 design features); 2013 exam, session 2, Q4 (location and functions of the refueling water tank and the severe-accident design, APR1400 vs OPR1000); and 2019 exam, session 2, Q3 (design improvements of Shin-Kori 5 and 6 compared with Shin-Kori 3 and 4, and their purposes). Typical questions ask for the main design features against the OPR1000, the IRWST and safety injection, severe-accident features, building layout and the later-unit improvements. For a session 1 short answer, points 1, 2, 4 and 6 of the summary are enough; for part (a) of the 2013 question, build the answer around the four functions of the IRWST (single water source, condensing POSRV discharge, water source for cavity flooding, protection from external hazards).
