Accidents, Safety Analysis & Severe Accidents · C-SA-026

Passive Autocatalytic Recombiner (PAR) — Catalytic Principle, Removal-Rate Correlations, Ignition Risk and the South Korean Replacement

A passive, self-starting catalytic device whose removal rate scales with hydrogen concentration and pressure, and which can itself ignite the mixture at high concentrations.

Concept C-SA-026
Definition

A passive autocatalytic recombiner (PAR) is a device inside containment that recombines hydrogen and oxygen into steam without a flame on a platinum or palladium catalyst surface. It starts by itself when hydrogen reaches it, with no power, signal or operator action, and the chimney draft created by the reaction heat keeps drawing in the surrounding gas.

This entry concentrates on the PAR as a single piece of equipment.

Principle and mechanism

Catalytic recombination and the chimney effect

A commercial PAR is a vertical box open at the top and bottom. The catalyst sits at the bottom (plates: AREVA, AECL; pellets: NIS) and the upper part is an empty chimney.

  1. The hydrogen–air(–steam) mixture enters through the bottom inlet and touches the catalyst surface.
  2. The reaction 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O takes place on the catalyst, releasing about 242 kJ per mole of hydrogen.
  3. The hot, hydrogen-depleted gas rises up the chimney and leaves at the top, and fresh gas takes its place.

Thanks to this self-feeding flow, no fan or power is needed, and the PAR works even in an SBO with both off-site and emergency power lost. The outlet flow also helps mix the containment atmosphere. The outlet gas is not pure steam but a hot mixture with less hydrogen and oxygen and more steam. If 0.01 mol of hydrogen reacts per mole of mixture, the adiabatic gas temperature rise is about 80 K (241.8 kJ/mol × 0.01 ÷ 29.5 J/mol·K, a teaching calculation with constant specific heat). The catalyst surface temperature is not derived from this value.

Start-up, shutdown and performance indicators

  • Start-up: commonly given as about 1–2 vol%, but it depends on the product and conditions. In the OECD/NEA THAI tests (2007–2009) it ranged from 0.2 to 4.4 vol% depending on conditions. Dry, high-pressure, high-temperature conditions gave early start-up; steam-saturated (condensing) conditions delayed it. Once heated, a PAR keeps running down to about 0.3 vol%.
  • Removal efficiency γ = (C_in − C_out)/C_in: 40–70 % (THAI, ample oxygen, depending on design). This is the concentration reduction in a single pass, not the removal efficiency for the whole containment.
  • Oxygen excess ratio Φ = 2·C_O₂/C_H₂: performance is maintained at Φ ≥ 2–3; at Φ = 1 it drops below 50 % of design capacity.

Removal-rate correlations

The removal rate is almost proportional to the inlet hydrogen concentration and increases with pressure, while the effects of steam and temperature are small (THAI). Analysis codes use the vendors' empirical correlations. The AREVA (now Framatome) plate-type correlation is as follows (as summarized in a KAERI paper implementing it in MELCOR, 2019):

  • R[kg/s] = η · x_min · (A·P + B) · tanh(100·x_min,lim) × 10⁻³
  • x_min = min(x_H₂, 2x_O₂, 0.08), x_min,lim = max(x_min − 0.005, 0), P [bar], η = 1 (x_H₂ ≤ x_O₂) or 0.6
  • FR1-380T: A 3.1, B 3.7; FR1-1500T: A 13.7, B 16.7

In dry air with ample oxygen, the large FR1-1500T removes about 1.21 g/s at 4 vol% and 1.0 bar, and about 1.49 g/s (5.4 kg/h) at 1.5 bar. The pressure dependence is the linear term A·P + B. The zero below 0.5 vol% and the constant value above 8 vol% are features of the model, not the actual start-up concentration or a capacity limit. The NIS correlation (Fischer 1995) has the form R ∝ x^1.307·P/T and is the default MELCOR model; the South Korean CERACOM correlation has the form k·x^1.1·P^1.2·(273/T)^1.5. A correlation gives the quasi-steady removal rate of a PAR that has already started; start-up delay and poisoning are accounted for separately.

Ignition risk

As the concentration rises, the catalyst temperature rises almost linearly. In THAI tests with ample oxygen, the PAR became an ignition source for the surrounding mixture under the following conditions. These are inlet concentrations and peak catalyst temperatures from specific tests and should not be used as universal thresholds.

ConditionInlet hydrogen concentrationPeak catalyst temperature
Dry (no steam)5.5–7.5 vol% (design-dependent)890–920 °C
Wet (45 % condensing steam)8–9 vol%960–1,005 °C

In the tests the flame spread upward from the PAR outlet and there was no flame acceleration. A South Korean evaluation (Hanul 1 and 2, 2024) also found that, with PAR-induced ignition included, the maximum flame acceleration index went from 0.891 to 0.793, below 1. When oxygen is short (Φ < 2.2), the catalyst does not get as hot and ignition is suppressed. Conversely, if spray rapidly removes steam from a steam-inerted atmosphere, ignition can occur at high concentration. In an answer, note that a PAR is a "flameless device" but can become an unintended igniter at high concentrations.

Inhibition and poisoning

  • Dust and volatile organic compounds (VOCs): accumulate on the catalyst during outages and degrade start-up performance (South Korean operating experience).
  • Water film (condensate): slows the initial heat-up and delays start-up.
  • Iodine and aerosols: potential poisons, but in the THAI aerosol-plus-iodine tests the efficiency stayed at 50–70 % with no major loss. The PAR can instead convert part of the CsI aerosol into volatile I₂, which is a source-term issue.
  • CO: produced during MCCI, and oxidized to CO₂ by the PAR. Suppression of low-temperature start-up has been suggested, but quantitative values depend on the design. CO in the mixture also increases detonation sensitivity.

What it means in a plant

Placement principles (MDEP 2021)

  • Distribute PARs in proportion to the local free volume, and place them high, above hydrogen release sources.
  • Arrange them so that PAR convection crosses compartment boundaries, and keep them away from walls.
  • Avoid missile and pipe-whip locations, and make the catalyst inserts easy to remove for inspection.
  • Choose locations from compartment-node hydrogen concentration analyses (South Korea: MAAP with about 40 compartments).
  • Size the number not only by total capacity but with dM_H₂/dt = ṁ_generation − ΣR_PAR − other removal, looking at generation rate, start-up delay, local accumulation and unavailability together.

APR1400 configuration (example, MDEP TR-APR1400WG-04 Rev. 4)

  • 30 PARs: 12 for design-basis accidents (8 large units at the top of the dome, 4 medium units at the IRWST vent stacks) + 18 for severe accidents.
  • The South Korean DBA analysis credits only 6 of the 12 (200 % capacity); DBA units are safety class 3, severe-accident units are non-safety class, and all are seismic Category I.
  • Catalyst performance uncertainty → the design calculation assumes a 25 % start-up failure (a different assumption from a 25 % performance reduction).
  • Technical specification surveillance: 25 % of each PAR's catalyst is sampled and tested at each planned outage.
  • Ten igniters back up rapid releases, and PAR-induced ignition is not currently considered in the design-basis analysis.

The South Korean PAR performance issue and replacement

DateDevelopment (based on NSSC agendas and press reports)
2012 onwardPARs installed progressively at operating plants as a Fukushima follow-up action
Jan 2021Whistleblower report: one vendor's PARs fell short of the licensee's purchase specification (0.2 g/s at 4 %)
Mar 2023173rd meeting: KAERI SPARC 4 % tests gave 0.131–0.137 g/s (vendor correlation 0.251); the design-basis criterion (average below 4 %) was still met
May 2024194th meeting: 8 % tests averaged 0.320 g/s (52 % of the analysis correlation value of 0.6146) → reanalysis with the licensing assumptions unchanged gave averages above 10 % at 14 of 18 units (maximum 13.61 %) → performance restoration required
Aug 2025220th meeting: operating licence amendment (FSAR and technical specifications) to replace the 50 affected PARs at 18 units with another vendor's product
2026Press reports of installation and performance checks of the replacement PARs during periodic inspections (Hanbit 6, Hanul 2)

The cause was the flow resistance of the catalyst grid structure, which slowed the natural-convection velocity (about 0.4 m/s against about 0.6 m/s for the comparison product). The NSSC judged that no emergency measures such as reactor shutdown were needed, because the frequency of accident sequences exceeding 10 % was 10⁻¹² to 10⁻¹³ per year and the concentration stays within 10 % when spontaneous burning by hot ex-vessel melt is considered, and it required the margin to be restored. The lesson is that vendor correlation values must be verified by independent tests. Analyses also use conservative assumptions such as an analysis correlation lower than the vendor's, 25 % performance degradation and "start-up 15 minutes after reaching 3 %".

When the questions were set (2010–2015), plants were switching from electric recombiners to PARs and Fukushima follow-up installation was under way. Today, combustible gases are evaluated as a mandatory challenge mechanism under the accident management notice, and the replacement after the performance issue was handled through an operating licence amendment.

"A recombiner sends hydrogen out of containment" is wrong. It turns hydrogen into steam inside containment.

Key points at a glance

  1. Definition: flameless recombination H₂ + ½O₂ → H₂O on a Pt/Pd catalyst; passive equipment needing no power or operator action.
  2. Principle: catalytic reaction heat (242 kJ/mol H₂) → chimney natural draft → self-feeding, promotes mixing.
  3. Start-up: typically about 1–2 vol% (0.2–4.4 % depending on conditions); keeps running to about 0.3 %.
  4. Performance: removal rate ∝ hydrogen concentration and pressure (AREVA correlation); efficiency 40–70 %; Φ ≥ 2 needed.
  5. Ignition risk: catalyst can become an ignition source at 5.5–7.5 % (dry) or 8–9 % (wet).
  6. Inhibition: dust and VOCs, water film, iodine and aerosols, CO, oxygen starvation.
  7. Placement: in proportion to free volume, high above release sources, convection between compartments.
  8. APR1400: 30 PARs (12 DBA + 18 severe accident), 25 % start-up failure assumed, backed up by 10 igniters.
  9. South Korea: performance shortfall confirmed → replacement approved in 2025; lesson of independent performance verification.

This is a model answer, not an official solution. It reflects the criteria current as of 2026.

Exam relevance

The PAR has appeared four times in the Korean Professional Engineer (Nuclear Power) exam: 81st exam, session 3, Q3 (hydrogen generation in containment, in the context of the containment hydrogen recombiner system); 2010 exam, session 3, Q2 (when igniters and PARs operate, and their advantages and disadvantages); 2012 exam, session 1, Q8 (function and operating principle of the PAR); and 2015 exam, session 2, Q2 (a comparison table of electric recombiners, igniters and PARs). The typical question types are the operating principle, and a comparison with electric recombiners and igniters by operating method, start-up timing, location, removal principle, advantages and disadvantages.
