Safe Shutdown Earthquake (SSE) and Operating Basis Earthquake (OBE) — Two Levels of Design Earthquake and the Shutdown Decision

SSE keeps safety functions, OBE decides whether the plant must shut down — defined as response spectra anchored at a peak ground acceleration.

Concept C-SA-012
Definition

The safe shutdown earthquake (SSE) is the maximum design ground motion for which structures, systems and components (SSCs) important to safety are designed to remain functional; the operating basis earthquake (OBE) is the ground motion for which the equipment needed for continued operation without undue risk to the public remains functional, and under current US rules it serves mainly as the criterion for shutting down and inspecting the plant.

Both are defined as horizontal and vertical design response spectra of the free-field motion at the site, and their size is quoted as the peak ground acceleration (PGA, in g) at which the spectrum is anchored. The "design basis earthquake (DBE)" that appears in exam questions is usually used with the same meaning as the SSE.

Under the former rule (OBE ≥ SSE/2), the allowable stresses for the OBE load combinations were strict, so in practice the OBE often governed the design rather than the SSE. Because of this excessive conservatism, 10 CFR 50 Appendix S (App. S, 1997) allowed the OBE design analysis to be omitted if the OBE is set at no more than 1/3 of the SSE. Even then, the OBE remains as the criterion for shutdown and inspection.

Design response spectra (RG 1.60)

A response spectrum is a curve of the maximum response of single-degree-of-freedom oscillators with a given damping to a ground motion, plotted against their natural frequency. RG 1.60 gives a standard shape normalized to a PGA of 1.0 g and a maximum displacement of 36 in, in the form of amplification factors at control points.

  • Above 33 Hz is the ZPA (= PGA); C→B→A is the acceleration region, D→C the velocity region, and below D the constant-displacement region (Sa ∝ f²).
  • Converting the displacement factor at point D to acceleration gives, horizontally, Sa = (2π·0.25)²·(2.05×36 in)/g ≈ 0.47 g.
  • The vertical/horizontal ratio is 2/3 at 0.25 Hz and below and 1 at 3.5 Hz and above. So in RG 1.60 the vertical ZPA equals the horizontal ZPA. "Vertical = 2/3 of horizontal" holds only in the low-frequency range.
  • The actual spectrum is scaled linearly by the site PGA (straight lines on log-log axes between control points, linear interpolation between damping values). For a 0.3 g SSE the horizontal peak (2.5 Hz) is 0.94 g, and with OBE = SSE/3 it is 0.31 g. This means that the spectral acceleration at 2.5–9 Hz is about 2.6–3.1 times the PGA; actual structural loads are obtained by structural response analysis.

For new US sites, the SSE is now set from the ground motion response spectrum (GMRS) based on probabilistic seismic hazard analysis (PSHA) under 10 CFR 100.23 and RG 1.208. RG 1.60 remains as the licensing basis of existing plants and as the shape of the "minimum input anchored at 0.1 g". The APR1400 certified seismic design response spectrum (CSDRS) is a high-frequency-enhanced shape based on RG 1.60, raised by 30 % around 25 Hz.

What it means in a plant

Design: seismic categories and load combinations

  • Seismic Category I: safety-related items. They must keep their safety functions at the SSE and, if the OBE is used as a design input, stay within allowable stresses under OBE plus normal loads.
  • Seismic Category II: items whose collapse or fall could damage Category I items. Only structural integrity at the SSE is required.
  • Seismic Category III: follow general industrial codes.

A design that eliminates the OBE (OBE ≤ 1/3 SSE) makes up for the lost conservatism with substitute requirements. The requirements of the South Korean guide KINS/RG-N04.29 are:

  • Seismic fatigue: 2 SSE events × 10 maximum stress cycles
  • Equipment seismic qualification: 5 events at 1/2 SSE + 1 SSE
  • Radioactive waste systems etc.: designed for 1/2 SSE
  • PSA-based seismic margin assessment (SMA): core damage HCLPF ≥ 1.67 × SSE

Values for South Korean reactor types

  • Operating plants (OPR1000 / Korean standard plant, etc.): SSE 0.2 g, OBE 0.1 g (= SSE/2, former scheme)
  • APR1400 (Saeul 1–4, Shin-Hanul 1–4): SSE 0.3 g, OBE = SSE/3
  • After the Gyeongju earthquake (2016): the upgrade of the seismic capacity of the safe-shutdown systems of operating plants to 0.3 g, begun as a Fukushima follow-up measure, was accelerated.
  • Vertical design value: with RG 1.60, the vertical ZPA of the Korean standard plant is also 0.2 g.

Operation: judging OBE exceedance and shutting down

App. S requires that if vibratory ground motion exceeds the OBE or significant damage occurs, the plant be shut down, and that before restart it be demonstrated that the equipment needed for continued operation has no functional damage and that the licensing basis is maintained. Earthquakes at South Korean sites are rich in high frequencies, so PGA alone says little about damage potential. For this reason the judgement also uses the response spectrum and indicators such as CAV.

  • US RG 1.166 (based on EPRI NP-5930 and NP-6695; the 2020 Rev.1 absorbed the restart guide RG 1.167): the OBE is exceeded only if both of the following checks are exceeded.
  • Response spectrum check: exceeded if the 5 % damped spectrum of any one of the three free-field components exceeds max(OBE Sa, 0.2 g) at 2–10 Hz, or max(OBE Sv, 6 in/s) at 1–2 Hz.
  • CAV check: standardized cumulative absolute velocity CAV > 0.16 g·s. For each 1-second window, if |a| exceeds 0.025 g at least once, ∫|a|dt over that window is added.
  • South Korea, KINS/RG-N04.18 Rev.2 (2017): the plant is shut down if either the PGA check (any of the three components exceeds the OBE PGA) or the response spectrum check (same conditions as in the US) is exceeded.
  • 2016 Gyeongju earthquake (M5.8): the measured PGA at Wolsong 1·2 was below 0.1 g (maximum about 0.098 g). However, the response spectrum exceeded the OBE (Unit 1 about 0.43 g at 7.3 Hz > 0.3 g), so Units 1–4 were shut down manually; inspection found no damage.

Separately, the automatic seismic trip system (ASTS) trips the reactor automatically above its setpoint (0.18 g per press reports).

Interpreting an exam case: the 2004 Uljin earthquake

This was a magnitude 5.2 earthquake offshore, with its epicentre 80 km away. The readings at the Uljin plant did not even reach the alarm setpoint of 0.02 g at the time. Being far below the Korean standard plant OBE of 0.1 g and SSE of 0.2 g, it had no safety impact, and the alarm setpoint was afterwards lowered to 0.01 g. Magnitude (energy) and site acceleration cannot be converted directly, because they depend on distance and ground conditions.

When these questions were set (2001–2009), the OBE = SSE/2 scheme was the standard for South Korea's operating plants. Today, designs that eliminate the OBE, such as the APR1400, are used together with PSA-based seismic margin requirements. After the Gyeongju earthquake, the post-earthquake shutdown judgement and seismic upgrades were strengthened.

Key points at a glance

  1. SSE: based on the maximum earthquake potential of the site; ground motion for which safety-important SSCs are designed to remain functional; horizontal PGA at least 0.1 g.
  2. OBE: earthquake expected during the lifetime; equipment needed for continued operation remains functional; today a shutdown and inspection criterion.
  3. Size: under current rules, analysis is waived if OBE ≤ 1/3 SSE (former rule ≥ 1/2 SSE).
  4. Spectrum: RG 1.60, control points 33, 9, 2.5 and 0.25 Hz, vertical/horizontal ratio 2/3 to 1, scaled by PGA.
  5. Seismic categories: I (functions kept at SSE), II (structural integrity), III (general codes).
  6. South Korea: operating plants SSE 0.2 g, OBE 0.1 g; APR1400 0.3 g; 0.3 g upgrades after Gyeongju.
  7. OBE exceedance: shutdown → inspection → show no functional damage before restart; US spectrum AND CAV 0.16 g·s, South Korea PGA OR spectrum.
  8. Making up for OBE elimination: fatigue 20 SSE cycles, HCLPF ≥ 1.67 SSE.

This is a model answer, not an official solution. It reflects the criteria current as of 2026.

Exam relevance

SSE and OBE have appeared six times in the Korean Professional Engineer (Nuclear Power) exam: 65th exam, session 4, Q6 (horizontal and vertical seismic design accelerations of the Korean standard plant); 71st exam, session 1, Q9 (DBE, design basis earthquake); 73rd exam, session 2, Q5 (definitions of SSE and OBE and the impact of the 2004 Uljin earthquake); 2009 exam, session 1, Q3; 2017 exam, session 1, Q9; and 2021 exam, session 1, Q7 (definitions of OBE and SSE). Most questions are short definitions, sometimes with a case (an actual earthquake) or the plant's design values. Do not write OBE = SSE/2 as the current rule — that is the former Appendix A; mention the 1/3 option of Appendix S, that the OBE remains the shutdown criterion, and that "vertical = 2/3 horizontal" applies only at low frequencies in RG 1.60.
