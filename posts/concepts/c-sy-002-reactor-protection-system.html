Systems & Equipment · C-SY-002

Reactor Protection System (RPS) and Plant Protection System (PPS) — Four-Channel 2-out-of-4 Coincidence Logic and Design Criteria

Four independent channels voted 2-out-of-4 open the trip breakers so the control rods fall by gravity, built to the single-failure, independence, testability and fail-safe criteria.

Concept C-SY-002
Definition

The reactor protection system (RPS) is a safety-class instrumentation and control system that opens the reactor trip breakers when a process variable reaches its trip setpoint, letting the control element assemblies fall by gravity, so that the specified acceptable fuel design limits (SAFDLs) and the reactor coolant pressure boundary limits are kept during anticipated operational occurrences. CE-type plants (OPR1000, APR1400) combine the RPS and the initiation functions of the engineered safety features actuation system (ESFAS) in a four-channel plant protection system (PPS).

The bases are Article 26 of the Korean technical standard rule ("Rule on Technical Standards for Reactor Facilities"), GDC 20–25 of 10 CFR 50 Appendix A, and 10 CFR 50.55a(h) (IEEE 603-1991).

In the analog CE design, when 2/4 is satisfied in any matrix, the matrix relay contacts de-energize all initiation relays K1–K4, and K1–K4 open the four trip breakers. When the CEDM power supplied from the MG sets is cut, the control rods fall by gravity. The ESFAS functions take the same bistable and 2/4 outputs and drive pumps and valves through the actuation logic of each train (A/B). In the APR1400, the ESFAS initiation signals from the LCL go to the ESF-CCS over safety-class high-speed links, and the ESF-CCS group controllers apply a selective 2/4 again.

The RTSS configuration differs by design. OPR1000 and Shin-Hanul 1 and 2 use four trip switchgear breakers with selective 2/4, while the APR1400 standard design (US design certification) uses two sets of four (eight breakers) with full 2/4. The PPS uses the undervoltage trip coils of the breakers, and the DPS uses the shunt trip coils of the same breakers (DCD Tier 2 Rev. 3, 7.2.1.3).

Two kinds of bypass

  • Channel (maintenance and test) bypass: the bypassed channel drops out of the logic and 2/4 becomes 2/3. Bypassing two channels at once is prevented by an interlock. Even in bypass, the reactor can still trip if one more of the remaining three channels fails to operate.
  • Operating bypass: deliberately removes a trip in ranges where it would interfere with operation, such as startup or low power (e.g. pressurizer low pressure, high logarithmic power). It must be removed automatically when the permissive conditions are no longer met.

Trip variables (APR1400 example)

Based on the digital protection system of Shin-Kori 3 and 4, there are 11 functions besides the manual trip (15 including the per-steam-generator duplicates).

The CPC takes the ex-core neutron flux, RCS temperatures and pressure, RCP speed and the CEA position deviation from the CEAC, calculates DNBR and LPD directly, and feeds its trip signals into the PPS bistables.

What it means in a plant

  • Difference from Westinghouse plants: in a Westinghouse design, the outputs of the process protection channels (two to four) are voted 2/3 or 2/4 by two SSPS trains, which open the series trip breakers RTA and RTB (with bypass breakers BYA and BYB for testing). DNB and overpower are prevented not by a CPC but by the variable setpoints overtemperature ΔT (OTΔT) and overpower ΔT (OPΔT). OTΔT is corrected for Tavg, pressurizer pressure and axial flux difference to prevent DNB; OPΔT is corrected for Tavg, its rate of change and the flux difference to prevent fuel overpower (kW/ft).
  • Trip breaker diversity: each breaker has both an undervoltage trip and a shunt trip. After the undervoltage trip devices failed and the automatic trip did not occur at Salem 1 in 1983, NRC GL 83-28 added automatic actuation of the shunt trip.
  • A new issue with digitalization: a software common-cause failure could disable all four channels at once, so a diverse protection system (DPS) and manual functions are provided.
  • Link to operation: trip setpoints enter the technical specifications as limiting safety system settings (LSSS), and the operability of protection channels is subject to LCOs.

When the 2011 question was set, the legal basis changed from the Atomic Energy Act to the Nuclear Safety Act and the technical standard rule became an NSSC rule, but the structure of the Article 26 requirements stayed the same. New plants have moved from analog matrices to digital PPS, and accordingly the requirement against software common-cause failure (Article 26(2)8) and the DPS have become key issues.

Coincidence logic is also easy to misread. 2/4 does not mean "trip when two channels fail"; it means "trip when two or more of the four channels of the same protective function are in the trip condition." Trips of different variables are not added together either. With 1/4, a single spurious signal causes a spurious trip; with 4/4, a single failed channel defeats the trip. 2/4 ignores one spurious signal while tolerating up to two channels that fail to operate.

Key points at a glance

  1. Definition: automatic reactor trip when a process variable reaches its setpoint; protects SAFDLs and RCS pressure limits during AOOs (Article 26(1), GDC 20).
  2. Structure: four measurement channels → bistables → 2/4 coincidence logic → initiation → trip breakers → CEAs fall by gravity.
  3. APR1400 PPS: RPS + ESFAS initiation; BP and LCL processors; ESF actuation through the ESF-CCS; the CPC supplies DNBR and LPD trip inputs.
  4. Redundancy: four channels; minimum redundancy kept with one channel out of service.
  5. Independence: physical and electrical separation between channels; isolation of safety and non-safety.
  6. Diversity: different variables detect the same event; undervoltage + shunt trip; DPS.
  7. Coincidence: 2/4 — prevents spurious trips from a single false signal, still works with a single failure.
  8. Testability: overlapping on-line tests with a channel in bypass (2/3).
  9. Fail-safe: the undervoltage (UV) path trips when de-energized → loss of power = trip (the shunt trip operates when energized).
  10. Other: separation from control systems, completion of protective action, automatic removal of operating bypasses; standards IEEE 603-1991 and 50.55a(h).

Exam relevance

The reactor protection system has appeared six times in the Korean Professional Engineer (Nuclear Power) exam: 2011 exam, session 1, Q1 (design criteria of the reactor protection system); 2017 exam, session 2, Q1 (design principles of protection and safety systems); 2020 exam, session 1, Q8 (protection system design criteria for reliability); 2021 exam, session 3, Q1 (redundancy, diversity, coincidence and testability, with examples); 2023 exam, session 2, Q2 (RPS, ESFAS and DPS); and 2026 exam, session 2, Q4 (APR1400 PPS composition, CPC functions and the ATWS types considered by the DPS). Four of the questions ask for design criteria and reliability principles, and two ask for system composition. Note that the Korean exam word for redundancy literally means "overlapping"; it is the same concept. Answer with the four-channel, 2/4 structure and give one concrete example for each criterion.
