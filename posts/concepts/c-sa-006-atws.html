Anticipated Transient Without Scram (ATWS) — Riding Out a Failure to Trip with Diverse Systems and Negative Reactivity Feedback

When the control rods do not go in: power–heat removal imbalance, peak RCS pressure, UET and the diverse systems that back up the reactor trip.

Concept C-SA-006
Definition

An ATWS (Anticipated Transient Without Scram) is an event in which an anticipated operational occurrence (AOO) occurs but the reactor trip function of the protection system fails, so the control rods do not insert; because it requires multiple or common-cause failures, it is classified as beyond the design basis (10 CFR 50.62(b)). The ATWT (Anticipated Transient Without Trip) of the 2013 question is the same concept. This note concentrates on the big picture: the ATWS phenomenon, acceptance criteria, regulatory requirements and the responses of different reactor types.

Principle and mechanism

Why it is dangerous — an imbalance between power and heat removal

The essence of an ATWS is the imbalance between core power and secondary-side heat removal. Transients that call for a trip are mostly events that reduce heat removal; if the trip fails, the power stays on and the imbalance becomes larger than in the AOO itself. There are two consequences.

  1. RCS overpressure: coolant temperature and volume rise → the pressurizer goes solid → safety valves discharge → pressure peak. Integrity of the reactor coolant pressure boundary is the main issue.
  2. Reduced fuel thermal margin: if flow and heat transfer deteriorate while power is maintained, DNB and cladding temperature become concerns.

Representative event — loss of normal feedwater (LONF)

The most limiting event for overpressure is loss of normal feedwater without a turbine trip. It proceeds as follows.

  1. Main feedwater is lost → SG level falls; a trip signal is generated but the rods do not insert
  2. The turbine keeps drawing steam, so the SG inventory is depleted quickly → secondary heat removal drops sharply
  3. Coolant temperature rises → volume expands → the pressurizer goes solid and the safety valves (four POSRVs in APR1400) discharge
  4. The negative reactivity feedback from the rising moderator temperature reduces power and the pressure peak forms
  5. Auxiliary feedwater restores heat removal → boron injection secures subcriticality

The peak pressure is therefore set by "how negative the MTC is" and by "the pressure-relief capacity and the timing of auxiliary feedwater and turbine trip". A turbine trip cuts off secondary steam extraction and can initially act to heat up the RCS. In an LONF ATWS, however, preserving the SG inventory and delaying dry-out has the larger effect, and in the APR1400 analysis the peak pressure was indeed lower in the case with a turbine trip.

Acceptance criterion and UET

US SRP 15.8 takes the peak RCS pressure in a PWR ATWS to be at or below the ASME Service Level C stress limit of about 3,200 psig (22.06 MPa(g)) (the value proposed in NUREG-0460). South Korea also uses KEPIC MN Level C, 22.06 MPa(g).

The MTC becomes more negative as burnup proceeds (boron dilution). So early in the cycle the peak pressure in the same event can exceed the criterion. This period is called the UET (Unfavorable Exposure Time), defined as "the period (fraction of the cycle) during which, given the plant condition and available systems, the MTC is such that the RCS pressure in an ATWS would exceed 3,200 psig". The NRC regards it as the same concept as the "unfavorable MTC" in the basis documents of the ATWS rule.

The generic Westinghouse analysis behind the rule (SECY-83-293) used a full-power MTC of −8 pcm/°F (more negative than this for 95 % of the cycle). In other words, the basis was that the MTC must be sufficiently negative for about 95 % or more of the cycle. Longer cycles (12 → 18 months) and power uprates raise the critical boron concentration and shift the MTC in the positive direction, which lengthens the UET. That turns into core design constraints such as more burnable absorber or adjusted loading patterns.

The APR1400 ATWS evaluation took cycle 1 as the limiting cycle because the initial-core MTC is less negative than that of the equilibrium core, and gave no credit for operator action for 30 minutes.

What it means in a plant

Mitigation systems by reactor type

Reactor typeSystemFunction
Westinghouse type (Kori, Hanbit 1 and 2, etc.)AMSACOn SG low-low level (or low main feedwater flow): turbine trip + auxiliary feedwater start. No reactor trip function; armed only above about 40 % power
CE type, OPR1000DSS + diverse turbine trip and auxiliary feedwaterOn high pressurizer pressure, removes power from the control rod drive mechanisms (diverse trip)
APR1400DPSReactor trip (high pressurizer or containment pressure), turbine trip (3-second delay after a PPS or DPS reactor trip signal), auxiliary feedwater actuation (low SG level), safety injection, control rod withdrawal prohibit

A 2004 KINS document classified Hanbit (Yonggwang) 3 and 4 and Hanul (Ulchin) 3 and 4 as "DSS + AMSAC", and South Korean literature sums this up as "DPS = AMSAC + DSS". The APR1400 DPS differs from the PPS and ESF-CCS in hardware, software and trip mechanism, so it covers not only ATWS but also software common-cause failure (CCF) of the digital safety I&C.

ATWS events considered by the DPS (APR1400 example)

The APR1400 ATWS evaluation report selected six heat-removal-reducing AOOs for qualitative evaluation: ① loss of external load ② turbine trip ③ loss of condenser vacuum ④ MSIV closure ⑤ loss of offsite power (loss of non-emergency AC power) ⑥ loss of normal feedwater. Of these, ②, ③, ⑤ and ⑥ (⑥ with and without turbine trip) were analyzed quantitatively, and loss of normal feedwater without a turbine trip was the most limiting. In the South Korean review of the accident management plans, the maximum ATWS RCS pressure for APR1400 (Saeul 1 and 2, Shin-Hanul 1 and 2) was 17.59 MPa(g), within the 22.06 MPa(g) criterion.

Operator response (EOP)

Once a failure to trip is confirmed, a manual trip is attempted immediately; if that fails, power to the control rod drive mechanisms (MG sets) is interrupted. Then turbine trip, auxiliary feedwater and emergency boration bring the core subcritical. Westinghouse plants handle this in function restoration procedure FR-S.1 (response to nuclear power generation/ATWS); CE and Korean standard plants handle it under "reactivity control", the first safety function of the standard post-trip actions. The specific steps follow each unit's EOPs.

Related accidents and transients

  • Salem Unit 1 (USA, 25 February 1983): despite an automatic trip signal, neither of the two reactor trip breakers opened, and the operators tripped the reactor manually about 30 seconds later. The cause was binding of the undervoltage trip attachments (wear from improper maintenance), and it was found afterwards that the same failure had occurred on 22 February. There was no core damage or release. It led to NUREG-1000 and Generic Letter 83-28 (trip breaker testing and maintenance, automatic shunt trip) and was the trigger for finalizing the ATWS rule.
  • Browns Ferry Unit 3 (USA BWR, 1980): water had collected in the scram discharge volume, so some control rods did not insert; insertion was completed with repeated manual scrams.

The history runs NUREG-0460 (1978) → Salem (February 1983) → SECY-83-293 → promulgation of 10 CFR 50.62 (26 June 1984, effective 26 July). The Korean rule differs from the US rule in requiring the reactor trip function for all reactor types. Compared with when the questions were set, two things have changed: the 2016 accident management plan system made ATWS an explicitly mandatory multiple-failure accident, and installation of AMSAC in South Korean Westinghouse-type plants has been approved since 2003.

ATWS and SBO (total loss of AC power) are both mandatory multiple-failure accidents, but ATWS is essentially a failure of reactivity control, while SBO is a loss of the power that drives heat removal.

Key points at a glance

  1. Definition: AOO + failure of the reactor trip function (rods not inserted); a beyond-design-basis event caused by multiple failures or CCF.
  2. Phenomenon: power–heat removal imbalance → coolant expansion → pressurizer goes solid → safety valves discharge → RCS pressure peak.
  3. Most limiting event: loss of normal feedwater (LONF) without turbine trip.
  4. Criterion: peak pressure at ASME Level C, about 3,200 psig (22.06 MPa(g)).
  5. Influencing factors: MTC (largest), pressure-relief capacity, timing of auxiliary feedwater and turbine trip.
  6. UET: the period with an MTC that would make the pressure exceed the criterion; concentrated early in the cycle.
  7. Regulation: 10 CFR 50.62 (1984) — diverse turbine trip and auxiliary feedwater for PWRs, DSS for CE and B&W / Korean technical standard rule, Article 27.
  8. Systems: AMSAC (Westinghouse), DSS (CE), DPS (APR1400).
  9. Response: manual trip → interrupt MG sets → turbine trip and auxiliary feedwater → emergency boration.

This is a model answer, not an official solution. It reflects the criteria current as of 2026.

Exam relevance

ATWS has appeared seven times in the Korean Professional Engineer (Nuclear Power) exam: 76th exam, session 4, Q6 (meaning of ATWS and function and principle of the mitigation systems); 2012 exam, session 1, Q2 (ATWS mitigation systems); 2013 exam, session 3, Q1 (ATWT: definition, events considered, influencing factors, UET, mitigation systems); 2018 exam, session 4, Q3 (definition and operating experience, regulatory requirements, measures by reactor type); 2021 exam, session 4, Q1 (definition, and the difference between Westinghouse-type and OPR/APR-type mitigation equipment); 2025 exam, session 2, Q4 (purpose, functions and output signals of the DPS in the Korean standard plant); and 2026 exam, session 2, Q4 (APR1400 PPS and CPC, and the six ATWS event types considered by the DPS). Typical questions ask for the definition, the overpressure mechanism and influencing factors (with UET), the 10 CFR 50.62 requirements, and a comparison of AMSAC, DSS and DPS. For "the six ATWS event types considered by the DPS", list the six AOOs of the APR1400 ATWS evaluation report, and remember that AMSAC does not trip the reactor.
