Operation, Regulation & Quality · C-OP-005

Safety Class — Classes Assigned by Safety Function and Their Link to Codes, Seismic Category and Quality

Function decides the class and the class brings the code: Class 1 for the RCPB, Class 2 for containment and emergency cooling, Class 3 for support, with seismic, electrical and QA classes judged separately.

Concept C-OP-005
Definition

A safety class is assigned to the equipment of a reactor facility according to the safety function it performs (integrity of the reactor coolant pressure boundary, safe shutdown and maintaining shutdown, and preventing or mitigating accidents that could cause off-site exposure). A common PWR scheme (for example, ANSI/ANS-51.1) divides equipment into Safety Classes 1, 2 and 3 and non-safety class, and each class carries its own design, fabrication and inspection codes.

The key is the order "function → class → code." What the equipment does decides its class, and the class then brings with it the applicable code (for example, ASME III Class 1, 2, 3) and the level of quality assurance.

Principle and mechanism

What separates the classes: the consequence of losing the function

Applying the highest code everywhere only raises cost without improving safety. Regulations therefore require — for example, GDC 1 (10 CFR 50 App. A) in the United States — classes and codes "commensurate with the importance of the safety function." One question separates the classes in a typical PWR scheme: if this equipment fails, which barrier or safety function is lost?

ClassAssigned to (summary)
Safety Class 1Pressure-retaining parts and supports of RCPB equipment whose failure could cause a loss of coolant exceeding the reactor coolant makeup capacity
Safety Class 2Containment pressure-retaining parts and supports + equipment, other than Class 1, for fission-product isolation, emergency heat and radioactive-material removal (spray), emergency subcriticality (adding negative reactivity or limiting positive reactivity addition through pressure-boundary equipment, e.g. boron injection), emergency core cooling (residual heat removal, ECCS) and emergency coolant supply and storage (refueling water tank)
Safety Class 3Equipment, other than Classes 1 and 2, for functions including hydrogen control, boron and coolant makeup, maintaining core geometry, shielding, spent-fuel cooling and support of safety equipment (cooling water, lubrication, diesel fuel, motive power, electric power, signals, control, interlocks, environmental conditions)
Non-safety classEquipment without the functions above; specific requirements may be imposed on radioactive waste treatment, effluent monitoring and so on

Three operating rules

  • The stricter class governs: if one component has functions of two classes, it takes the higher one.
  • The class break is usually at isolation valves: the shared region between different classes is treated as the higher class, but becomes the lower class if an interaction analysis demonstrates it. The class downstream of a valve is judged again by the function of that section. The RCPB ends at the outer containment isolation valve for lines that penetrate containment, at the second of two valves closed during normal operation for lines that do not, and it includes the RCS safety and relief valves (10 CFR 50.2).
  • The makeup-capacity condition: piping (mainly small-bore lines and instrument lines) whose failure loses coolant within the reactor coolant makeup capacity (charging pumps), so that the plant can be shut down and cooled normally, is not Class 1 even though it is part of the RCPB. The criterion is this condition, not size itself. In the United States, for example, the corresponding Class 1 exception in 10 CFR 50.55a(c)(2) is worded in terms of the "makeup system during normal operation."

Correspondence with other classification schemes

The safety class usually corresponds with the quality group, code, seismic category, electrical class and quality assurance. However, each classification is judged by its own criteria, so they do not become identical automatically.

  • ANSI/ANS-51.1 (PWR): SC-1, SC-2, SC-3 and NNS are the same framework as Safety Classes 1, 2, 3 and non-safety, and are used in FSAR classification tables.
  • RG 1.26 quality groups A–D: A = RCPB (ASME III Class 1, governed directly by 10 CFR 50.55a), B = Class 2, C = Class 3, D = general codes such as B31.1 and Sec. VIII.
  • RG 1.29 seismic category: safety-class equipment is normally Seismic Category I. Even non-safety equipment gets Seismic Category II, to assure structural integrity, if its collapse could damage Category I equipment.
  • IEEE 308 Class 1E: electrical equipment essential to emergency shutdown, containment isolation, core cooling, heat removal and prevention of release. Some national class schemes group safety electrical and I&C items under Safety Class 3 support functions (motive power, electric power, signals, control). In design documents electrical items are marked separately in an electrical-class column (1E/Non-1E).
  • Quality assurance: Safety Class 1, 2 and 3 items get the full 10 CFR 50 App. B quality assurance programme (utility quality class Q).

What it means in a plant

Classes can split within one component

The class is assigned per SSC according to its safety function, and different parts (pressure boundaries) of the same component can carry different classes. Typical examples from APR1400 design documents:

  • Safety Class 1: reactor vessel, pressurizer, steam generator primary side (channel head and tubes), reactor coolant pump casing, hot and cold legs, pressurizer surge line, control element drive mechanism pressure housing, reactor vessel supports
  • Safety Class 2: containment pressure-retaining parts, steam generator secondary shell, safety injection pumps and safety injection tanks, containment spray system, shutdown cooling system, in-containment refueling water storage tank (IRWST), main steam and main feedwater piping up to the isolation valves, fuel transfer tube flange
  • Safety Class 3: component cooling water (CCW), essential service water (ESW), spent fuel pool cooling system, core support structures, emergency diesel generator fuel oil and support systems, 1E power and protection systems, main control room HVAC, hydrogen control equipment
  • Non-safety: turbine-generator, condenser, main feedwater pumps, circulating water system, radioactive waste treatment systems

In the same steam generator, the inside of the tubes and the channel head (primary side) are Class 1 while the shell outside the tubes (secondary side) is Class 2. The control element drive mechanism pressure housing is Class 1, while its other parts fall into Classes 2 and 3 or non-safety.

Uses in design and operation

  • Design: the class determines material certification, design stress analysis (including fatigue analysis for Class 1), non-destructive examination and hydrostatic test pressure.
  • Operation: the scope and interval of in-service inspection, keeping the class of maintenance parts (equivalency and commercial-grade dedication) and the equipment scope of the Technical Specifications all follow the class.
  • Accident response: Safety Class 1 prevents damage to the first barrier (LOCA), Class 2 mitigates that accident, and Class 3 supports Class 2 so it can work. A higher class number does not mean less important: lose CCW or ESW and the ECCS eventually cannot reject its heat.

  • Safety-related vs important to safety: safety-related SSCs perform the three functions in 10 CFR 50.2 and correspond to Safety Class 1, 2 and 3 equipment. SSCs important to safety are a wider set that includes non-safety items such as Seismic Category II, fire protection and ATWS and SBO equipment.
  • Utility quality classes: utilities also assign a quality class for the level of QA. Safety-related items (Q) get the full App. B programme; non-safety items with augmented quality requirements (e.g. Seismic Category II equipment, radioactive waste treatment systems under RG 1.143) get augmented quality; the rest get general industrial quality.
  • 10 CFR 50.69 RISC-1 to 4: a US scheme that overlays risk significance on the deterministic class to adjust the scope of special treatment. It does not replace the deterministic safety classes.

Key points at a glance

  1. Definition: a class assigned to equipment according to its safety function (RCPB integrity, safe shutdown and maintaining it, preventing and mitigating accidents with off-site exposure) (for example, US GDC 1, ANSI/ANS-51.1; IAEA SSG-30).
  2. Safety Class 1: RCPB equipment whose failure would lose coolant beyond the reactor coolant makeup capacity (reactor vessel, pressurizer, SG primary side, RCP casing, main coolant piping).
  3. Safety Class 2: containment pressure-retaining parts + isolation, spray, boron injection, ECCS, residual heat removal, IRWST.
  4. Safety Class 3: support functions — CCW, ESW, spent-fuel cooling, core support structures, EDG fuel oil, safety electrical and I&C (1E).
  5. Non-safety: turbine, condenser, main feedwater pumps; specific requirements for radwaste systems.
  6. Rules: the stricter class governs; shared regions take the higher class; piping meeting the makeup-capacity condition is excluded from Class 1.
  7. Codes: 1, 2, 3 ↔ ASME III Class 1, 2, 3 ↔ quality groups A, B, C; non-safety ↔ D (B31.1).
  8. Links (judged separately): safety-class equipment is normally Seismic Category I; electrical and I&C items are Class 1E; QA is App. B (utility quality class Q).
