Accidents, Safety Analysis & Severe Accidents · C-SA-005

Containment Failure Modes — Bypass, Isolation Failure, Early and Late Failure, Basemat Melt-Through and the Seven Threats

Bypass, isolation failure, early and late failure and basemat melt-through, from damage mechanisms to release categories and regulatory threats.

Concept C-SA-005
Definition

Containment failure modes are the types of path and timing by which the containment loses its function as a barrier against the release of radioactive material in a severe accident: bypass, isolation failure, early failure, late failure and basemat melt-through. The key point is that the term covers not only cases where the structure breaks, but also cases where the structure is intact yet the barrier was never closed (isolation failure) or the release never passes through the barrier at all (bypass).

Principle and mechanism

Two classification axes: path and timing

Failure modes are sorted by release path (does the release pass through the containment atmosphere?) and timing (before or after reactor vessel failure?). The earlier the failure, the less chance aerosols have to deposit or be scrubbed, and the more likely a release before the public has been evacuated, so the risk is larger. NUREG-1150 (1990) grouped release categories on this basis into bypass, isolation failure, early, late and intact. The five types below are a representative teaching classification; in an actual PSA they overlap or are subdivided further depending on the plant and the purpose of the analysis (BMT is sometimes grouped with late failure).

In South Korean PSA practice, a sequence in which containment heat removal fails and the containment fails by overpressure before the reactor vessel fails is sometimes treated separately as containment failure before reactor vessel breach (CFBRB, etc.). The classical WASH-1400 (1975) notation — α (in-vessel steam explosion), β (isolation failure), γ (hydrogen combustion), δ (overpressure) and ε (melt-through) — also appears often in exam material. Later studies assessed the probability of α-mode failure as very low.

Damage mechanisms by mode

  1. HPME/DCH: if the vessel fails while the RCS is at high pressure, melt is ejected as fine particles that heat the containment atmosphere directly; together with hydrogen combustion the pressure rises sharply.
  2. Hydrogen combustion and detonation: deflagration of hydrogen from the Zr–steam reaction and MCCI, flame acceleration, and the dynamic loads of deflagration-to-detonation transition (DDT).
  3. Steam explosion (FCI): after the melt fragments, the vapor films collapse at once and a shock wave forms. An in-vessel explosion raises the concern of an upper-head missile; an ex-vessel explosion loads the cavity structure.
  4. Overpressure and high temperature: decay-heat steam and CO, CO₂ and H₂ from MCCI accumulate and pressurize the containment slowly; high temperature degrades penetration seals.
  5. MCCI and BMT: the melt decomposes and erodes the concrete, releases gases and penetrates the foundation.
  6. Bypass: steam generator tube rupture, or creep rupture of tubes by natural circulation of hot gas in a high-pressure, dry SG; failure of a low-pressure system boundary (ISLOCA).
  7. Rocket mode: the reaction force of a high-pressure vessel failure moves the vessel and damages penetrating piping.

What it means in a plant

Use in Level 2 PSA

Level 1 core damage sequences are grouped into plant damage states (PDS), followed through the headings of the containment event tree (CET) (bypass? → isolation → RCS pressure at vessel failure → early failure → cavity cooling → late failure), and the end states are grouped into release categories (STC, source term categories). Branch probabilities are set by overlaying the load distribution on the containment fragility curve (conditional failure probability as a function of pressure). The RCS pressure at vessel failure (depressurized or not) is not a heading that decides failure directly; it is a state variable that changes the branch probability of early failure such as DCH. End states are assigned to release categories again by release timing, magnitude and scrubbing, and failure modes and release categories are not one-to-one. Early failure, bypass and isolation failure are the main contributors to LERF (large early release frequency). The South Korean notice's frequency target for releases exceeding 100 TBq of Cs-137 is a separate metric, defined differently from LERF.

Pressure margin: the APR1400 example

The APR1400 containment design pressure is 60 psig (about 0.515 MPa absolute). In the review of the accident management plans (January 2025; Saeul 1 and 2, Shin-Hanul 1 and 2), the maximum severe accident pressure was assessed at 0.60 MPa (88 psia) and the allowable pressure at 0.85 MPa (123.7 psia). Maximum MCCI erosion was 0.183 m, less than the roughly 1.20 m of concrete above the liner, and the POSRVs were assessed to depressurize the RCS from 17.25 MPa to 1.72 MPa (absolute) or below, preventing HPME/DCH. 0.60 MPa is the assessed load and 0.85 MPa the acceptance criterion of that assessment; neither is an ultimate failure pressure. The likelihood of failure is evaluated separately with the ultimate pressure capacity and the fragility curve.

What has changed since the questions were set

  • When the 2010 and 2016 questions were set, accident management was not a statutory document; since June 2016 the accident management plan system has listed the threats in a notice.
  • NSSC Notice No. 2017-34 in the 2022 question is now Notice No. 2026-1, and the threat table has moved from Appendix 2 to Appendix 3 (same content).
  • The "conditions for ensuring integrity" in the 78th exam centered on the design basis of the time (design pressure, leak rate, isolation); today they also include severe accident performance goals (barrier maintained for 24 hours, release frequency target).

Using the design pressure (60 psig) as the failure pressure is wrong. Failure is judged with the ultimate pressure capacity and fragility curve, which are far above the design pressure. BMT progresses most slowly and follows the basemat path, so it is generally not the mode with the largest atmospheric release. Bypass skips only the scrubbing inside containment; underwater scrubbing on the SG secondary side and similar effects are assessed separately.

Key points at a glance

  1. Definition: types of path and timing by which the containment loses its barrier function in a severe accident.
  2. Bypass: SGTR (spontaneous, or thermally induced creep), ISLOCA — bypasses scrubbing in containment; can be large and early.
  3. Isolation failure: open isolation valves or penetrations — managed by LLRT and isolation signals.
  4. Early failure: HPME/DCH, hydrogen detonation, steam explosion (α), rocket mode.
  5. Late failure: overpressure by steam and non-condensable gases, high temperature — hours to days.
  6. BMT: foundation erosion by MCCI — very slow, soil path.
  7. PSA: PDS → CET → release categories; early failure and bypass dominate LERF.
  8. Regulation: NSSC Notice No. 2026-1, Appendix 3 — seven mandatory threats + additional ones; barrier maintained for 24 hours after core damage.
  9. APR1400: POSRV, HMS, CFS, ECSBS; assessed load 0.60 / acceptance criterion 0.85 MPa.

Exam relevance

Containment failure modes have appeared six times in the Korean Professional Engineer (Nuclear Power) exam: 78th exam, session 4, Q6 (conditions for ensuring PWR containment integrity); 2010 exam, session 4, Q2 (potential factors that can damage containment, and containment bypass); 2016 exam, session 2, Q4 (types of reactor building damage and their mechanisms); 2022 exam, session 1, Q3 (severe accident phenomena that damage containment, short answer); 2022 exam, session 4, Q5 (containment integrity threats after significant core damage under the NSSC notice, and the APR1400 mitigation features); and 2026 exam, session 4, Q4 (mandatory and additional threats after significant core damage). Typical questions ask for the classification of failure types with their mechanisms, the list of threats in the notice with the matching APR1400 features, and bypass as a distinct path. Quote the current notice (No. 2026-1, Appendix 3), and do not present the design pressure as the failure pressure.
