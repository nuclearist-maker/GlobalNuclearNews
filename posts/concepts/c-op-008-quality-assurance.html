Operation, Regulation & Quality · C-OP-008

Nuclear Quality Assurance (QA) — The 18 Criteria of 10 CFR 50 App. B, NQA-1, CFSI and Commercial-Grade Dedication

More than inspection: independence, objective evidence and a graded approach, and why a test certificate still needs its own authenticity check.

Concept C-OP-008
Definition

Nuclear quality assurance (QA) comprises all the planned and systematic actions necessary to provide adequate confidence that safety-related structures, systems and components (SSCs) will perform satisfactorily in service. In the United States, for example, it is required through the 18 criteria of 10 CFR 50 App. B; internationally, IAEA GSR Part 2 sets the management-system requirements.

Quality control (QC) — checking that the physical characteristics of an item meet requirements — is part of QA. QA is not inspection alone but a management system that ties together organization, design, procurement, records and audits.

Principle and mechanism

The 18 criteria follow an item through its life

The 18 criteria of App. B (Criterion I–XVIII) state "what must be controlled." How to do it is set by the licensee in its QA manual and procedures. They are easiest to remember when laid on the flow of an item being designed, purchased, fabricated, installed and operated.

Three working principles

  • Independence: inspections and audits are carried out by people without direct responsibility for the work. This is why Criterion I requires the QA organization to have authority free of cost and schedule pressure.
  • Objective evidence: the acceptance condition is not "we made it" but "records show it was made to requirements." That is why records (XVII) and audits (XVIII) close the loop.
  • Graded approach: the rigor of the requirements is matched to the safety significance of the item. Utility quality classes — safety-related (full App. B), augmented quality and general — are an example.

What it means in a plant

Supply-chain control: commercial-grade dedication and CFSI

With parts becoming obsolete and suppliers dwindling, commercial items made outside an App. B program often have to be used in safety-related service. 10 CFR 21.3 defines this as commercial-grade dedication (CGD): an acceptance process in which critical characteristics are identified by safety-function analysis and verified, giving reasonable assurance that the item will perform its intended safety function. The acceptance methods are basically the four of EPRI NP-5652.

  1. Special tests and inspections
  2. Commercial-grade survey (verifying the supplier's process controls)
  3. Source verification (witnessing at the manufacturing site)
  4. Item and supplier performance record (not used alone; combined with another method)

Once dedication is complete, responsibility for Part 21 defect reporting passes to the organization that performed the dedication.

CFSI stands for counterfeit items (imitating genuine products), fraudulent items (supplied with false markings or false documents) and suspect items (showing signs of either). Substandard items are sometimes grouped with them. A suspect item is not a confirmed counterfeit, so it is segregated and held, then judged by investigation and evaluation. Even when a test certificate exists, the authenticity of that document must be checked separately: document-based acceptance (Criteria VII and XVII) presumes that the documents are genuine.

  • App. B vs NQA-1: App. B is the regulatory requirement (what); NQA-1 is the consensus standard for meeting it (how). The NRC endorses specific editions through RG 1.28.
  • Part 21 reporting vs nonconformance control: Part 21 requires reporting of defects and failures to comply in basic components to the regulator; Criteria XV and XVI govern how nonconforming items are identified, dispositioned and corrected within the QA program.

Key points at a glance

  1. Definition: all planned and systematic actions giving confidence that safety-related SSCs will perform satisfactorily (includes QC).
  2. Basis: for example, 10 CFR 50 App. B (US) and IAEA GSR Part 2 (management system).
  3. 18 criteria: (1) organization (2) QA program (3) design control (4) procurement documents (5) instructions, procedures and drawings (6) document control (7) purchased items and services (8) identification (9) special processes (10) inspection (11) test control (12) measuring and test equipment (13) handling, storage and shipping (14) inspection, test and operating status (15) nonconforming items (16) corrective action (17) QA records (18) audits.
  4. Principles: independence, objective evidence, graded approach.
  5. Standards: ASME NQA-1 (endorsed by RG 1.28 Rev. 6).
  6. Supply chain: CGD = verification of critical characteristics (tests, survey, source verification, performance record); CFSI = blocking counterfeit, fraudulent and suspect items, including checking the authenticity of documents.
  7. Scope: QA is wider than inspection — inspection and test (X, XI) are only two of the 18 criteria.
