In-Service Inspection (ISI) — The 10-Year Interval and Three Periods, Examination Categories, Flaw Disposition and Risk-Informed ISI

How operators find aging flaws before failure: the 10-year interval, examination categories and methods, flaw acceptance and evaluation, and the risk-informed approach.

Concept C-OP-010
Definition

In-service inspection (ISI) is the set of nondestructive examinations, pressure tests, repairs and replacements, and evaluations of unexpected operating events that the operator carries out to monitor and evaluate how far safety-related equipment has degraded with time during plant operation; it includes the pre-service inspection (PSI) performed before operation.

In South Korea the definition is in Article 3(1) of NSSC Notice No. 2026-14, "Notice on In-Service Inspection of Reactor Facilities". Its parent basis is Article 63 (testing, surveillance, inspection and maintenance) of the Korean technical standard rule for reactor facilities, and the technical codes are KEPIC MI or ASME Section XI.

Principle and mechanism

Why look again and again

The pressure boundary is designed and fabricated to ASME III quality, but in service it is subject to aging degradation: fatigue, stress corrosion cracking (SCC), flow-accelerated corrosion (FAC), boric acid corrosion and neutron irradiation embrittlement. ISI is the system that finds the flaws this degradation produces before failure, sizes them and decides whether they are acceptable. It is built in three stages.

  1. Pre-service inspection (PSI): before first operation, the same methods and scope as ISI are applied to record a baseline. Later indications are judged against it to tell fabrication flaws from service-induced flaws. PSI is repeated after repair or replacement.
  2. In-service inspection: within the 10-year inspection interval, specified locations are examined by specified methods, spread over the interval.
  3. Flaw disposition: comparison with acceptance standards → repair/replacement or analytical evaluation → successive and additional examinations.

The 10-year interval and three inspection periods

Article 4 of the Korean notice requires ISI in units of 10 years, with the interval starting on the date of commercial operation. For pressurized heavy-water reactors only the first interval is 5 years or less. ASME XI Inspection Program B (IWB-2412) divides the 10 years into three periods ending at cumulative years 3, 7 and 10, and requires at least 16 %, 50 % and 100 % of the examinations to be complete (cumulatively) by the end of each period. In practice the examinations are spread over the planned refueling outages. The Class 1 system leakage test (B-P, VT-2), however, is done at every refueling outage.

There are three families of methods. Volumetric examination (UT, RT) finds flaws within the wall thickness; surface examination (PT, MT, ET) finds surface and near-surface flaws; visual examination comprises VT-1 (surface condition: cracks, wear, corrosion), VT-2 (evidence of leakage during a pressure test) and VT-3 (structural condition: deformation, gaps, displaced parts). UT may be performed only with procedures and personnel whose flaw-detection capability has been demonstrated on test specimens (ASME XI Appendix VIII); Article 11 of the Korean notice makes this mandatory as performance demonstration for UT and for steam generator tube ECT.

Article 6(1) of the Korean notice requires one thing more than ASME XI. Safety Class 1 piping butt welds of nominal 2 inches and larger that are not isolated from the RCS in normal operation get an additional volumetric examination even where a surface examination is the requirement.

When a flaw is found

  • The indication is sized and compared with the IWB-3500 acceptance standards (Table IWB-3410-1). If it is within them, it is accepted.
  • If it exceeds them, the component is either repaired or replaced (IWA-4000), or an analytical evaluation (IWB-3600) predicts the flaw growth (fatigue, SCC) to end of life to decide whether it is acceptable. For ferritic vessels the representative criteria are KI < KIa/√10 for normal and upset conditions and KI < KIc/√2 for emergency and faulted conditions.
  • A flaw accepted for continued service by analysis is re-examined in each of the next three inspection periods (IWB-2420). If the evaluation is not satisfied, the route is repair or replacement. In a category where a flaw exceeding the standards is found, additional examinations (IWB-2430) expand the sample, and if more are found, all remaining items are examined.

What it means in a plant

  • Design: accessibility and inspectability must be secured at the design stage (GDC 32, 10 CFR 50.55a(g)(3)). Locations that cannot be examined become candidates for alternatives or relief requests under Article 13.
  • Operation: the long-term in-service inspection plan (LTP) goes to the NSSC 3 months before the start of the 10-year interval, and the results of each outage campaign are reported to the designated agency (KINS) within 3 months of completion. A summary of how flaws exceeding the standards were dispositioned is due before criticality (Articles 8 and 9).
  • Safety: ISI is the in-service means of confirming the integrity of the reactor coolant pressure boundary (RCPB). Together with leak detection it is one pillar of the leak-before-break (LBB) argument.
  • Operating experience: steam generator tubes follow the SG program in the Technical Specifications (ECT, plugging at 40 % through-wall) under the limitations in the notice's annex. Reactor vessel head penetrations and dissimilar-metal welds received augmented examinations under Code Cases N-729 and N-770 because of PWSCC experience.

Risk-informed ISI (RI-ISI)

Deterministic ISI examines a fixed share of welds evenly, as in B-J. RI-ISI assigns each piping segment a risk = failure potential (degradation mechanism) × consequence of failure (CCDP, CLERP) and moves the examinations to high-risk locations.

  • Basis: RG 1.174 and 1.178, SRP 3.9.8, and RG 1.200 for PSA quality. The methodologies are EPRI TR-112657 and WCAP-14572, Code Cases N-560, N-577 and N-578, the streamlined N-716, and ASME XI Appendix R.
  • EPRI method: a consequence rank (CCDP boundaries at 10⁻⁴ and 10⁻⁶) and a failure-potential rank (FAC high; thermal fatigue and SCC medium; no mechanism low) give risk categories 1 to 7. At least 25 % of the elements in each high-risk category (1 to 3) and 10 % in each medium-risk category (4 and 5) are selected for examination (element counts rounded up). Low-risk elements drop out of the RI-ISI sample, but pressure and leak tests and separate degradation programs such as FAC remain.
  • Conditions: the change in risk must meet the RG 1.174 acceptance guidelines, and defense in depth and safety margins must be maintained. What changes is "where and how much". The examination method is chosen to suit the degradation mechanism, and acceptance is still evaluated against the ASME XI requirements.
  • The "Risk-based Inspection" of the 2002 question is the petrochemical RBI term (API 580/581). For nuclear plants, answer in terms of RI-ISI.

The revision history matters too. The Korean notice went from Ministry of Science and Technology Notice No. 2004-13 through Ministry of Education, Science and Technology Notice No. 2009-23 to the NSSC notices. When the questions were set in 2009, 2019 and 2022, its title was "Regulation on In-Service Inspection…"; on 26 August 2026, No. 2026-14 renamed it "Notice on…" and widened the applicable editions to KEPIC MI 2020 and ASME XI 2019. In the United States, the July 2024 revision replaced "120-month interval" with "code of record interval". A licensee using the 2017 Edition or later can keep its code of record for up to two intervals. The inspection interval itself is still 10 years.

The periodic inspection that the regulator performs every refueling cycle is a regulatory inspection under Korea's Nuclear Safety Act; ISI is an inspection performed by the operator.

Key points at a glance

  1. Definition: the operator's nondestructive examinations, pressure tests, repair/replacement and event evaluations that monitor and evaluate the degradation of safety-related equipment with time in service, including PSI (Article 3 of the notice).
  2. Basis: Korean technical standard rule Article 63, NSSC Notice No. 2026-14, KEPIC MI / ASME XI, 10 CFR 50.55a(g).
  3. Scope: Safety Class 1, 2 and 3 components (IWB, IWC, IWD), supports (IWF), containment Class MC and CC (IWE, IWL).
  4. Interval: 10 years (starting at commercial operation; PHWR first interval 5 years); three periods at cumulative 3, 7 and 10 years with minimums of 16, 50 and 100 %; B-P leak test at every refueling.
  5. Methods: volumetric (UT, RT), surface (PT, MT, ET), visual (VT-1, VT-2, VT-3); performance demonstration for UT and ECT.
  6. Flaws: IWB-3500 acceptance standards → if not met, repair/replacement or IWB-3600 evaluation → if kept in service, re-examination in the next three periods; additional examinations.
  7. Procedure: LTP 3 months before the interval starts; results reported within 3 months; code edition = the one in effect 1 year before the interval starts.
  8. RI-ISI: risk = failure potential × consequence; 25 % of high-risk and 10 % of medium-risk elements; change in risk within the acceptance guidelines (RG 1.174).

This is a model answer, not an official solution. It reflects the criteria current as of 2026.

Exam relevance

ISI has appeared four times in the Korean Professional Engineer (Nuclear Power) exam, all as short-answer items in session 1: the 67th exam (2002), session 1, Q1 ("risk-based inspection program"); 2009 exam, session 1, Q6 ("in-service inspection"); 2019 exam, session 1, Q11 (ISI scope and interval for light-water reactors); and 2022 exam, session 1, Q7 (the definition of "in-service inspection"). Typical questions ask for the legal definition, the scope and the 10-year interval with its three periods, and the risk-informed approach. In 2009, in-service testing was asked in Q5 of the same session, so the first scoring point is not to mix up ISI and IST. For the 2002 question, answer in terms of RI-ISI rather than the petrochemical RBI.
