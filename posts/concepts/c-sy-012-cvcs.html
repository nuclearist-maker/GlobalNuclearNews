Systems & Equipment · C-SY-012

Chemical and Volume Control System (CVCS) — The RCS Auxiliary System That Handles Inventory, Boron and Water Chemistry Through Letdown and Charging

Letdown, purification and charging: the flow balance that holds pressurizer level, the VCT, boron control and the boron dilution event.

Concept C-SY-012
Definition

The chemical and volume control system (CVCS) is an auxiliary system that continuously draws off part of the RCS coolant (letdown), purifies and adjusts it, and returns it (charging), so as to manage the coolant inventory (pressurizer level), the boron concentration and the primary water chemistry. It also supplies RCP seal injection and pressurizer auxiliary spray.

Principle and mechanism

Functions and subsystems

The KINS training material (based on Uljin 3·4) lists the functions as follows.

  1. Inventory control: adjust charging and letdown to follow the pressurizer level program (e.g. 33–52.6 %). Let down excess coolant during startup and power increases, charge during shutdown and power reductions, and make up normal leakage.
  2. Boron concentration control: compensate for burnup, xenon and temperature changes and secure shutdown margin (boration and dilution).
  3. Water chemistry and purification: pH (LiOH), dissolved oxygen (hydrazine when cold, hydrogen at power), filtration and ion exchange.
  4. RCP seal injection: 6.6 gpm per pump; the controlled bleed-off is returned to the VCT.
  5. Pressurizer auxiliary spray: divert part of the charging flow when the RCPs are stopped and the differential pressure for normal spray is lost.
  6. Monitoring and other duties: boronometer, process radiation monitor (trend of cladding damage), pressurization for leak tests, purification of shutdown cooling flow, removal of noble gases through VCT venting.

The number of subsystems differs between references. The same training text divides the system into four (letdown and charging / seal injection and auxiliary spray / makeup and boric acid makeup / boron recovery) in one place and into seven (letdown, charging, seal water supply, chemical addition, makeup, boron recovery, boric acid makeup) in another. In an answer, state the basis of the division you use.

Flow path and pressure and temperature changes

The job of the letdown system is to bring the 2,235 psig, 565 °F coolant down to the 60 psig and 120 °F that the purification equipment can tolerate (OPR1000 example).

  1. RCS cold-leg letdown nozzle → letdown isolation valves
  2. Regenerative heat exchanger (tube side): gives heat to the charging water and cools the letdown to 395 °F. Preheating the charging water prevents thermal shock at the nozzle and the positive reactivity insertion of cold water.
  3. Letdown flow control valve: adjusts flow on a signal from the pressurizer level control system and reduces pressure to 460 psig.
  4. Letdown heat exchanger (component cooling water): cools to 120 °F.
  5. Back-pressure control valve: holds the upstream pressure at 460 psig (above the saturation pressure at 450 °F of about 422 psia ≈ 408 psig → suppresses boiling in the heat exchanger and flashing at the valve); about 60 psig downstream.
  6. Filter (2 μm) → purification ion exchanger (mixed bed) or deborating ion exchanger → spray into the top of the VCT.
  7. Charging pumps → regenerative heat exchanger (shell side) → RCS cold-leg charging nozzle, with branches to seal injection and auxiliary spray.

The normal balance is letdown 72 gpm and charging 88 gpm (61.6 to the RCS + 26.4 seal injection). Of the seal injection, 10.4 gpm enters the RCS and 16 gpm returns to the VCT as controlled bleed-off. On the RCS side, 61.6 + 10.4 = 72 gpm in equals 72 gpm out.

Volume control tank (VCT)

The VCT is the surge tank that receives the letdown. At Uljin 3·4 it holds 4,917 gal with a design pressure of 75 psig and an external pressure of 15 psig. Normal operation is 20 psig and 120 °F.

  • Level buffer: the normal level band (33–44 %) alone absorbs RCS expansion and contraction during power changes without automatic makeup, with about 500 gal of margin for pure water during dilution.
  • Level interlocks: automatic makeup starts at 34 % and stops at 44 %; at the 60 % high level, letdown bound for the VCT is diverted to the boron recovery system; at the 5 % low-low level, charging suction transfers to the refueling water tank.
  • Gas control: in operation a hydrogen cover gas keeps RCS hydrogen at 15–50 cc/kg (suppressing oxidizing species from radiolysis); at shutdown the tank is purged with nitrogen; vent gas goes to the gaseous waste system.
  • It provides suction head for the charging pumps, and its overpressure relief valve (75 psig) discharges to the equipment drain tank.

Boron control and the boric acid concentrator

For boration, borated water from the refueling water tank (4,000–4,400 ppm) is used; for dilution, pure water from the reactor makeup water tank. They are mixed at a blending tee and sent to the VCT or the charging pump suction. There are four makeup modes: automatic, borate, dilute and manual. Near the end of the cycle, when boron falls to around 30 ppm, the amount of dilution water rises exponentially, so boron is removed directly with the deborating ion exchanger.

The boric acid concentrator heats recovered low-concentration borated water with auxiliary steam and evaporates it. The water leaves as vapor and is condensed (10 ppm B or less; to the makeup water tank after anion exchange), while the boric acid, being essentially non-volatile, stays at the bottom as a 4,000 or 21,000 ppm concentrate. A degasifier upstream removes dissolved gases first.

21,000 ppm (ppm B, by mass of solution) is 2.1 w/o B, which as boric acid is 2.1 × 5.72 ≈ 12.0 w/o H₃BO₃. The solubility of boric acid is about 4.5 wt % at 20 °C, so a 12 wt % solution must be kept above about 60 °C to prevent precipitation. The boric acid batching tank in the training text is also heated to 155 °F (68 °C).

An APR1400 normal-condition example is letdown 80 gpm, charging 93 gpm and seal injection 6.6 gpm per RCP (KEPCO E&C 2010 analysis conditions). The seal injection heat exchanger and the orifice-bypass preheating line were removed, and a letdown isolation valve inside containment was added. The auxiliary charging pump can be run on the alternate AC source during an SBO and was evaluated as a diverse means of RCP seal cooling.

Operation and safety

  • Startup (cold): remove oxygen with hydrazine below 121 °C → form the pressurizer steam bubble → add hydrogen to the VCT after the RCPs start → dilute before criticality.
  • Shutdown: purge the VCT with nitrogen, borate to the cold-shutdown concentration, and depressurize with auxiliary spray after the RCPs stop.
  • Lithium management: lithium builds up through B-10(n,α)Li-7 and is removed by the purification ion exchanger. Coordinated B–Li operation lowers lithium as boron decreases to keep the high-temperature pH constant.
  • Water chemistry limits (old technical specification 3/4.4.7 structure): dissolved oxygen 0.10 ppm steady-state and 1.00 ppm transient (for Tavg above 250 °F); chloride and fluoride 0.15 and 1.50 ppm each. If a steady-state limit is exceeded for 24 hours or a transient limit is exceeded, the plant must be in hot standby within 6 hours and in cold shutdown within 30 hours. The concern is stress corrosion cracking of stainless steel (by chloride and fluoride, accelerated when oxygen is present).

Boron dilution event

A boron dilution event is one in which pure water enters at maximum flow because of a makeup control error or a valve failure. Assuming complete mixing and inflow equal to outflow, C(t) = C₀·exp(−Qt/V), and the time to reach the concentration at which shutdown margin is lost is t = (V/Q)·ln(C₀/Ccrit). With training assumptions (unborated inflow, constant volume, complete mixing, constant density) of V = 300 m³, effective dilution flow Q = 132 gpm (0.500 m³/min), C₀ = 1,000 ppm and Ccrit = 900 ppm, t = 600 × 0.105 ≈ 63 minutes. If the alarm sounds at t = 0, this comfortably exceeds the 15-minute SRP criterion. The OPR1000 and APR1400 alert the operator with a boron dilution alarm that monitors a rise in startup-range neutron flux.

Key points at a glance

  1. Definition: an auxiliary system that manages RCS inventory, boron concentration and water chemistry through letdown and charging.
  2. Functions: pressurizer level control, boration and dilution, pH (LiOH) and oxygen (H₂, N₂H₄) control, purification, RCP seal injection, auxiliary spray.
  3. Letdown: cold leg → regenerative HX → flow control valve (460 psig) → letdown HX (120 °F) → back-pressure valve → filter and ion exchangers → VCT.
  4. Charging: VCT → charging pumps → regenerative HX → cold leg, with a seal injection branch.
  5. Balance example: letdown 72, charging 88 (= 61.6 + 26.4), controlled bleed-off 16 gpm.
  6. VCT: H₂ cover gas (15–50 cc/kg), N₂ purge, makeup at 34/44 % level, diversion at 60 %, charging NPSH.
  7. Boric acid: factor 5.72; 21,000 ppm B = 12.0 w/o H₃BO₃; the concentrator separates water from boric acid by evaporation.
  8. APR1400: centrifugal charging pumps, BAST, HPSI connection removed.
  9. Safety: boron dilution 15/30 minutes; isolation valves (SIAS, CIAS).

Exam relevance

The CVCS has appeared four times in the Korean Professional Engineer (Nuclear Power) exam: 67th exam, session 2, Q6 (number of CVCS subsystems and five functions); 2011 exam, session 4, Q2 (principle of the boric acid concentrator and conversion of 21,000 ppm to w/o); 2013 exam, session 2, Q3 (reactor coolant water chemistry limits and CVCS control of pH and dissolved oxygen); and 2020 exam, session 1, Q2 (design bases of the volume control tank). For the 2020 VCT question, the VCT section above (capacity and design pressures, level buffer, level interlocks, gas control, charging-pump suction head and relief) can be written as it is. For the 2013 question, the contrast between startup (hydrazine) and normal operation (hydrogen, LiOH) under "Operation and safety" is the backbone of the answer. In conversion questions, state that ppm B is by mass of solution and show the 5.72 factor (H₃BO₃ 61.83 / B 10.81).
