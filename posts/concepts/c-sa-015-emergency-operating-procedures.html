Accidents, Safety Analysis & Severe Accidents · C-SA-015

Emergency Operating Procedures (EOP, Symptom-Based) — The Dual Event-Based and Symptom-Based Structure with SPTA, ORP and FRP

Event-specific recovery when the diagnosis is right, function-based recovery when it is not — the dual structure adopted after TMI.

Concept C-SA-015
Definition

Emergency operating procedures (EOPs) are the procedures with which main control room operators, after a reactor trip or safety injection, confirm and restore safety functions and bring the plant to a stable state in design-basis accidents and in multiple-failure situations before core damage. Current EOPs combine event-based procedures, which diagnose the cause of the accident and respond to it, with symptom-based procedures, which judge the safety functions from measured symptoms and restore the threatened functions first.

In South Korea, the vendor's generic guidelines are called "emergency operating guidelines (EOGs)", and the unit-specific procedures written from them are called "emergency operating procedures".

Principle and mechanism

From event-based to symptom-based — the lesson of TMI

In the 1979 TMI-2 accident, the pressurizer power-operated relief valve (PORV) stuck open. Because the pressurizer level was high, the operators concluded that the system was full of water and throttled high-pressure safety injection. In fact coolant kept escaping, and about half of the core melted. The accident showed that an approach which first names the accident and then follows that accident's procedure breaks down when the diagnosis is wrong or failures overlap.

The NRC required reanalysis of accidents and improvement of the EOPs under item I.C.1 of NUREG-0737 (1980), and set out how to write them in NUREG-0899 (August 1982). The sequence is technical guidelines → procedures generation package (PGP) → writer's guide → verification and validation (V&V) → training. The owners' groups of each reactor type wrote the technical guidelines: the ERGs for Westinghouse plants and the CEN-152 EPGs for CE plants. In accepting CEN-152 Rev.1 in 1983 (GL 83-23), the NRC judged that its structure — "optimal recovery guidelines for simple events, functional recovery guidelines for everything else" — met the requirement for symptom-based response.

Flow of the Korean standard (CE-type) EOP

The EOPs of the South Korean OPR1000 and APR1400 follow the CE-type structure. Taking the Hanbit 3·4 procedures as an example, the flow is as follows.

  1. Standard post-trip actions (SPTA): right after the trip, the nine safety functions are checked and initial recovery actions taken. If all are satisfied, go to the reactor trip procedure; if even one is not, go to diagnosis.
  2. Diagnostic action (DA): the event is identified with a diagnostic flow chart. If a single event is identified and an ORP applies, go to that emergency procedure. If there are multiple events or diagnosis is impossible, go to the recovery procedures. Cautions note not to ignore early indications and not to diagnose an SGTR from radiation alarms alone.
  3. Seven optimal recovery procedures (ORPs): reactor trip, LOCA, SGTR, excess steam demand event (ESDE), total loss of feedwater, loss of offsite power and forced circulation, and station blackout (SBO). While an ORP is carried out, the safety function status check (SFSC) is repeated. If it is not satisfied, re-diagnose and, if needed, move to the recovery procedures.
  4. Functional recovery procedures (FRPs): each safety function has a resource assessment tree, listing the means of achieving the function as success paths. For reactivity control the success paths are CEA insertion → CVCS boration → SIS; for heat removal, SG cooling → once-through cooling (feed and bleed). The SFSC status is satisfied, challenged or jeopardized, and recovery is carried out in the order jeopardized → challenged → satisfied.
  5. Then move to long-term actions after stabilization, or transfer to the SAMG if its entry conditions are met.

Westinghouse ERGs

Diagnosis in E-0 (reactor trip or safety injection) leads to E-1 (loss of reactor or secondary coolant), E-2 (faulted SG isolation), E-3 (SGTR), ECA-0.0 (loss of all AC power) and so on. Separately, six critical safety function status trees (CSFSTs) are monitored. The priority is subcriticality (S) → core cooling (C) → heat sink (H) → RCS integrity (P) → containment (Z) → inventory (I). A red path is an extreme challenge, so the procedure in progress is stopped and the corresponding FR procedure entered. An orange path is entered when there is no red. For example, a core exit temperature (CET) of 1,200 °F (649 °C) or more is a red core-cooling path, leading to FR-C.1. While ECA-0.0 is being performed, the CSFSTs are only monitored, and transitions to FR procedures follow the instructions of that procedure.

What it means in a plant

List of safety functions — differences by reactor type

Questions such as the 2013 one ask for the functions by reactor type. The nine of the Korean standard design are reactivity control, maintenance of vital auxiliaries (power), RCS inventory control, RCS pressure control, core heat removal, RCS heat removal, containment isolation, containment temperature and pressure control, and containment combustible gas control. The Hanbit 3·4 recovery procedures combine core and RCS heat removal into one procedure (HR), giving eight. How functions are grouped can differ by unit and reactor type. Westinghouse has no power CSF: power is treated as a common support condition for several functions and handled by event procedures such as ECA-0.0.

Post-Fukushima improvements and computerization

When the 2016 amendment of Korea's Nuclear Safety Act created the accident management plan system, the Korean standard and APR1400 EOGs were improved to reflect multiple failures and extreme hazards. Six EOGs were revised, and MACST operating guidelines (MOGs) and one shutdown emergency operating guideline were newly written. The MOGs were introduced as 13 at the time of development; the current KHNP list has 14, including RCS makeup during shutdown (OECD/NEA 2023). Feed and bleed was added to the total-loss-of-feedwater guideline so that it can be carried out directly without entering functional recovery. The SBO guideline was linked with the three-phase ELAP MACST strategy.

The APR1400 (from Saeul 1·2) performs EOPs on the computerized procedure system (CPS) at the operator workstations. The CPS provides the step flow, related parameters, links to control displays and monitoring of continuous-action steps, and all operators share the progress. If all workstations are lost, safe shutdown and EOP execution continue from the safety console.

Links with SGTR and MSLB

Event-specific strategies belong to each ORP. For an SGTR, the core is the three- or four-step strategy of identifying and isolating the ruptured SG → cooling with the intact SGs → depressurizing to equalize pressure → terminating SI; the "three-step operating strategy" of the 76th exam refers to this. For an ESDE, the key points are isolating the faulted SG and managing PTS during overcooling.

Upper-head void during a LOCA (2013 exam, session 2, Q1)

With natural circulation or the RCPs stopped, flow in the reactor vessel upper head stagnates and the head stays hot. If the RCS is then depressurized, the head fluid falls below its saturation pressure and flashes. The symptoms are an abnormal rapid rise in pressurizer level during depressurization or spray, and a falling upper-head level indication (RVLIS). The EOP responds by repressurizing, restarting an RCP when conditions allow, adjusting the cooldown and depressurization rates, and venting through the reactor coolant gas vent system (RCGVS). Venting is stopped when the upper-head level recovers, the pressurizer level limit is reached, the subcooling criterion is reached, or the containment hydrogen concentration limit is reached.

When the questions were set (2013), EOPs centred on design-basis accidents. Current EOPs include some of the nine multiple-failure events of the accident management plan and are linked with the MOGs. SAMGs have also become a component of the legally required accident management plan.

Transfer to the SAMG is not the normal end of an EOP but a conditional branch. South Korean SAMGs use a CET of 649 °C or more together with failure of EOP core-cooling recovery as entry criteria, and the detailed judgement follows each unit's guideline.

Key points at a glance

  1. Definition: operator procedures that restore and maintain safety functions after trip or SI in DBAs and in multiple failures before core damage.
  2. Background: TMI-2 misdiagnosis → NUREG-0737 I.C.1, NUREG-0899 (1982).
  3. Dual structure: event-based ORPs + symptom-based SPTA, SFSC and FRPs.
  4. Flow: trip → SPTA (9 safety functions) → DA → 7 ORPs / FRPs → long-term actions.
  5. SFSC: satisfied, challenged, jeopardized; recover jeopardized first; repeated during ORPs.
  6. FRP: resource assessment trees and success paths (e.g. CEA → CVCS → SIS).
  7. Westinghouse ERG: E-0 → E-1 to E-3, ECA-0.0; 6 CSFSTs S, C, H, P, Z, I; red and orange paths.
  8. Boundary: transfer to SAMG when CET ≥ 649 °C (common in South Korea) AND core-cooling recovery fails.
  9. Improvements: multiple failures and extreme hazards reflected, MOGs (now 14), feed and bleed added.
  10. Computerization: APR1400 CPS; safety console if it is lost.

This is a model answer, not an official solution. It reflects the criteria current as of 2026.

Exam relevance

EOPs have appeared five times in the Korean Professional Engineer (Nuclear Power) exam: 76th exam, session 2, Q4 (SGTR detection equipment and the three-step operating strategy); 2013 exam, session 2, Q1 (upper-head void while performing the LOCA EOP); 2013 exam, session 2, Q6 (symptom-oriented procedures, purpose and types of CSFs for Westinghouse and OPR plants, generic issues); 2021 exam, session 1, Q12 (overview and types of safety functions in the OPR and APR EOGs); and 2021 exam, session 1, Q13 (definitions of EOP, SAMG and accident management plan). Questions range from short definitions to medium essays on structure. Distinguish the nine Korean standard safety functions from the six Westinghouse CSFs, and do not write that symptom-based means no diagnosis — diagnosis runs alongside. The "generic issues" of the 2013 question are a term from the technical basis documents of the South Korean EOPs; examples are RCP trip and restart criteria, natural-circulation verification, upper-head voiding, SI termination and throttling criteria, cooldown rate and PTS limits, initiation of feed and bleed, SG overfill, and instrument corrections for adverse containment conditions.
